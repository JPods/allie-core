# Government Stovepiping — Data and Accountability

**Established:** 2026-07-30
**Source:** Bill, during CrashHarvester county-level completeness work

## The Pattern

Governments stovepipe accountability and data by jurisdiction. When we work with government data, always ask:

1. **Who owns this data?** City? County? State? Federal?
2. **Do they allocate resources to publish it?** Or do they abandon it to another jurisdiction?
3. **Is there a fiscal consequence for not publishing?** If not, they won't.
4. **Where are the gaps?** Partial coverage is the norm, not the exception.

## Why This Matters for Every JPods Program

- **CrashHarvester:** OK DOT publishes OKC crash data but not Tulsa. The state collects it statewide but only publishes for some counties. No one is accountable for the gap.
- **MeshMobility:** City planners don't think about logistics, waste, or proximity. Those are other departments. The data lives in silos.
- **WebClerk:** Business licensing is city. Tax is state. Employment is federal. No single entity has the complete picture.
- **CityRoadkills:** Fatal crash data is federal (FARS). All-severity is state. Road maintenance is city. Accountability is nowhere.

## The Design Response

When the system encounters a data gap:

1. **Don't dead-end.** Search actively for the data.
2. **Detect the jurisdiction.** County, not just state. The gap is always at a specific boundary.
3. **Track the gap.** Every missing county is a signal. Aggregated signals drive acquisition priorities.
4. **Let users contribute.** They may know sources the system doesn't. n² connections.
5. **Follow up.** Alice submits data requests. The more people who ask, the faster governments publish.

## The Principle

Government stovepiping is sovereign immunity applied to data. No fiscal consequence for gaps means gaps persist. The fix is the same as every-injury: make the gap visible, make it a fiscal risk, and let the Wisdom of the Many force accountability.

This applies to every government interaction in every program. Always ask: who is accountable for this data, and what happens if they don't publish it?
