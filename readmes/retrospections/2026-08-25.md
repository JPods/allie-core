## Lessons for Allie

1. **Zero new models solved 10 industry gaps** — the instinct to create a new model for every problem is the industry default. WC3's architecture (signed quantities, status gates, JSON envelopes) eliminates the need. Before proposing a new model, ask: can an existing document with a negative quantity, a new status, or a JSON envelope handle this? Domain: [WC3]

2. **Auto-population is dangerous** — silently creating relationships behind the user's back (auto-setting sibling role FKs) was built and correctly rejected. The system can suggest, never assume. Reports recommend; users decide. Domain: [Cross-domain]

3. **Alice caught a real design issue** — when asked about shipping costs, Alice correctly identified that `totals.shipping` (customer charge) and `shipping.costs.actual` (carrier cost) are different concerns. This validates the pattern of asking Alice substantive questions, not just teaching her conclusions. Domain: [WC3]

4. **Administrative drag measurement** — `dt_requested`/`dt_response` on approval assignees makes the cost of bureaucracy visible. This is the right framing: not punishing slow approvers, but making the cost visible so the org can decide if the gate is worth the drag. Domain: [WC3]
