## 5. Business Structure — Isolated Risks

JPods uses the same capital structure as housing and hotels: a construction company builds, sells to an operating company, the operating company refinances with long-term debt. Risks are isolated by function, by state, and by city.

| Entity | Mission | Customer | Capital Type | Risk |
|--------|---------|----------|-------------|------|
| **JPods LLC** | Invention, standards, certification, training | NCCs and LMCs | Tech fees + $3M licenses | Innovation only |
| **JPods Capital LLC** | Aggregate transactions, interface to capital markets | NCCs and LMCs | Service fees | Financial service |
| **NCCs** (state-level) | Build networks within state laws | LMCs | Construction: $13M/mile, 18-36mo | Construction — isolated per state |
| **LMCs** (city-level, ~100K pop) | Operate networks, sell trips + cargo | Farebox payers | Asset: $20M/mile, 20-30yr bonds | Operating — isolated per city |

**JPods LLC does not raise capital for operations.** It is an invention company. Its mission is to "find the 10,000 ways not to make a lightbulb so 10,001 creates a lightbulb." If dominated by accountants and lawyers, it would stop at 2 and they would sue each other.

JPods LLC must survive based on NCCs and LMCs being willing to pay for its innovations. It must compete to provide services. This ensures market discipline.

**Why many NCCs and LMCs?**
- Diverse legal jurisdictions require local skills
- Granting equity to many people in localized companies empowers many
- The task is huge — $2.7 trillion/year in the US alone
- Monopolies are not durable
- Diverse efforts spread risks

**Weekly accounting cycles:** NCCs close every Wednesday at 3:00 PM (52 cycles/year). Nothing waits for the first of the month. Each week, guideways built by the NCC and certified by JPods are sold to the LMC. Fast cycling.

## 6. The Market

### 6.1 The Problem
Traffic costs Americans about **$2.7 trillion per year**. 95% of the energy is wasted moving 2 tons to move a person. Cars are 188 times less efficient per passenger-mile than freight railroads. 70% of Americans live paycheck to paycheck, and a typical new car costs $11,577 per year to own.

### 6.2 The Solution
Grade-separate to remove repeated start-stop. Suspend the vehicle from a guideway to cut vehicle mass. Costs drop **92%** (10× benefit). Warehouse robots on the scale of airports and malls.

### 6.3 Middle Mile / Last Mile
JPods is the **Middle Mile** — the WiFi of the Physical Internet. Station-to-station, neighborhood-to-neighborhood.

Last Mile modes — walking, bikes, scooters, Uber, golf carts — are the Bluetooth. They bridge the gap from JPods stations to front doors.

**~1 mile of JPods guideways per 14 miles of roads.** By removing 10,000+ cars, JPods makes streets safe to walk and bike. If JPods rates are too high, people use the safe streets JPods created — the network self-regulates through market discipline.

### 6.4 Viability Rule
**If we cannot remove 10,000 cars per day from a road network, we do not build.** Target corridors: 30,000+ AADT (Annual Average Daily Traffic). Dense niches first: airports, malls, campuses, stadium districts.

### 6.5 City Fiscal Benefits (not counted in projections)
- Reduced road maintenance (fewer heavy vehicles)
- Increased property tax from walkable streets (20× tax revenue per acre vs parking)
- Reduced crash costs and emergency response
- Healthier residents
- Parking lot conversion to productive land

### 6.6 Conservative Assumptions
Our projections do **not** count:
- Electrical generation revenue (1 MW per mile of guideway)
- Cargo revenue (continuous flow replaces weekly trash collection, enables fresh waste sorting)
- Advertising revenue
- City fiscal benefits
- Data/computing revenue (hive computing on station chips)

All are upside.

## 7. Risk and Mitigation

### Primary Risk: Regulatory
The Congressional study (1975) identified *"institutional failures"* as the barrier — not technical failure. For 50 years, government monopoly on transportation infrastructure blocked innovation.

### Mitigation Strategy
1. **Private Property First** — deploy on established private properties (malls, airports, campuses). No public right-of-way needed initially.
2. **Familiar Infrastructure** — frame as structural, solar, and electrical improvements. Same permit process as solar canopies.
3. **Certification of 4 guideways** in 4 jurisdictions establishes precedent.
4. **Legislative momentum** — laws passed in MA and NJ, votes in DFW and OK, contract in GA.
5. **ASTM F24 compliance** — amusement ride safety standard. 3,000× safer than roads.

### Worst Case Floor
