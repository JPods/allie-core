What the Internet Did for Information, JPods Does for Transportation

Digital Internet

Before the internet, information moved in big batches — newspapers, broadcast TV, scheduled mailings. Centrally planned. One size fits all.

The internet broke information into small packets, routed them through distributed networks, and delivered exactly what each person needed, when they needed it.

Result: everyone became a publisher. Information costs dropped 1000x. Innovation exploded.

Physical Internet

Today, traffic jams, oil supply shocks, and streets unsafe to walk and bike result from batch processes on analog roads. Electrical outages as the century-old analog grid breaks during storms.

JPods' patent for solar-powered transport networks digitizes and personalizes both energy and mobility — small pods (up to 500 kg) routed through distributed networks above traffic, on demand.

Result: everyone empowered to be mobility and energy self-reliant.

Regulations Are the Issue

For a century we knew the Internet was practical, but federal monopolies blocked innovation between 1918 and 1991 — free markets were not restored until The High-Performance Computing Act of 1991.

"When wireless is perfectly applied the whole earth will be converted into a huge brain… We shall be able to communicate with one another instantly, irrespective of distance. Not only this, but through television and telephony we shall see and hear one another as perfectly as though we were face to face, despite intervening distances of thousands of miles; and the instruments through which we shall be able to do this will be amazingly simple compared with our present telephone. A man will be able to carry one in his vest pocket."

— Nikola Tesla, Collier's magazine, 1926

1942: Hedy Lamarr patented the technology behind WiFi and Bluetooth. The Navy rejected it until 1957–62. Bluetooth did not commercialize until 1997.

1968: The Mother of All Demos documents most aspects of the Internet and modern computing. Start at minute 30 for 4 minutes to watch the "mouse," video conferencing, clicking on URLs — everything we use daily, demonstrated 57 years ago.

The same pattern is repeating with transportation. The technology exists. Congress identified it in 1975. Morgantown has run injury-free since 1975. The barrier is regulatory, not technical. We're breaking it — city by city, maker by maker.

5X5 Free Market

5X5 Free Market does for energy and transportation what the High-Performance Computing Act did for communications — restores a free market:

  • Privately funded transport networks 5 times more efficient than roads.
  • Pay 5% of gross transport revenues to use airspace over existing public Rights of Way to deploy solar-powered networks.
  • Regulated by the same standard as theme park thrill rides — a 3,000 times better safety record than DOT roads.

5X5 replaces top-down government planning with bottom-up liberty to innovate. The roles of government and innovators are restored:

Governments are essential to minimize violence from war and crimes. Governments coerce compliance with law — innovation is a compliance failure.

Two aspects of liberty innovate the general welfare:

  • Tolerance of Disruptive Minorities offering choices.
  • Tolerance of the Wisdom of the Many sorting choices in free markets and free speech.

Why 10x?

There is a 10X rule that to drive a paradigm shift requires a 10 times improvement.

In 1916 every US city over 10,000 people had one or more privately funded streetcar networks providing Mobility As A Service (MaaS). There were 260,000 miles of railroads. Top-down government planning since 1916 replaced the liberty to innovate and Wisdom of the Many sorting innovations:

  • Streetcar networks were wiped out.
  • 45% of 470 ton-mpg railroads were replaced by roads with the 25 mpg efficiency of the Model-T. Warren Buffett on the efficiency of railroads.

Railroads are 188 times more efficient than roads, but top-down government planning — with no retrospection on the consequences — replaced 45% of railroads.

Similarly, in 1935, 50% of US electricity was from renewables. The wind industry was making 80,000 windmills a year. The Rural Electrification Act of 1936 destroyed the renewable industry and blocked for a century achieving what Thomas Edison noted as practical in 1910:

Sunshine is spread out thin and so is electricity. Perhaps they are the same. This scheme of combustion to get power makes me sick to think of it. It is so wasteful. It is just the old, foolish Prometheus idea, and the father of Prometheus was a baboon.

When we learn how to store electricity, we will cease being apes ourselves; until then we are tailless orangutans. You see, we should utilize natural forces and thus get all of our power. Sunshine is a form of energy, and the winds and the tides are manifestations of energy… Do we use them? Oh, no! We burn up wood and coal, as renters burn up the front fence for fuel. We live like squatters, not as if we owned the property.

There must surely come a time when heat and power will be stored in unlimited quantities in every community, all gathered by natural forces. Electricity ought to be as cheap as oxygen…

— Thomas Edison, 1910

JPods Are 10x

JPods patent 6,810,817 is for solar-powered transport networks:

  • Robots on grade-separated guideways: "A method of controlling a transportation System for moving people, freight, and any combination whereof using a distributed network of intelligent devices without requiring the aid of a human driver"
  • Networks are solar-powered: "The method… providing… Solar and wind power generators integrated into the physical Structure of Said transportation System…."

JPods implementation:

  • Overhead guideways, grade separation, and automation removes repetitive applications of power required of cars on roads.
  • Suspended vehicles removes 75% of the parasitic mass (mass we burn energy to move that is not payload).
  • Solar collectors deployed over the guideways gather 40,000 vehicle-miles of power per mile per day. This is 4 times the energy required to payback the network.
  • Better Physics results in JPods being 10X more efficient than cars.
  • Watt-hours/passenger-mile: freight-railroads 5.5, bikes 15, JPods 78, walking 116, motorcycles 545, commuter rail 881, cars 1,032, buses 1,245, and taxis 4,204.
  • Watt-hours/passenger-km: freight-railroads 3.4, bikes 10, JPods 47, walking 73, motorcycles 339, commuter rail 548, cars 641, buses 774, and taxis 2,613.

Three layers to the Physical Internet — similar to the Internet's fiber optics (high speed), Bluetooth (last device), and WiFi (local area):

  • High speed and heavy lift between cities: airplanes, freight railroads, and tube mobility.
  • Last-mile: bikes, walking, scooters, proximity, Uber…
  • JPods are the middle-mile — between neighborhoods, not in them. Most places in cities will be within about a 15-minute walk of a JPods mesh network.

The 10X reduction in costs and energy were identified by Congressional Study PB-244854, "Automated Guideway Transit", as the solution to the 1973 Oil Embargo. It also noted the need for the 5X5 Free Market regulatory change:

"Institutional failures" had blocked such innovations for "four to six decades."

Energy and Mobility Self-Reliance

The Digital Internet made everyone a publisher. The Physical Internet makes everyone energy and mobility self-reliant. Solar panels on the guideways generate the power. The network is locally owned, locally governed, locally maintained. No foreign oil. No central authority deciding who moves and when.
