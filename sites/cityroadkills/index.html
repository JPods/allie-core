Stop de Kindermoord

In 1971, 450 children were killed on roads in the Netherland sparking, "STOP DE KINDERMOORD" — Stop the Child Murder.

What the Dutch Did

Parents and children occupied accident blackspots. Streets were closed so children could play safely. Thousands laid down with their bicycles outside the Rijksmuseum, pretending to be dead. They cycled with an organ to Prime Minister den Uyl's house, singing songs demanding safer streets. The government listened. Child road deaths fell from 450 in 1971 to 14 in 2010 — a 97% reduction.

Sources: Streetsblog · The Guardian · BBC · London Cycling Campaign · Dutch Reach Project · Halifax Examiner · Medium · EJ Atlas · Palo Alto Safe Routes to School

What America Did

Nothing. The same year, America had similar per-capita roadkill rates to the Netherlands, Sweden, and Denmark. All four nations shared the same car-centric policies. After the 1973 Oil Embargo, Europe changed course. America doubled down on highways, borrowed trillions for foreign oil, and fought oil wars. Today, the U.S. pedestrian death rate is 4× higher than the Netherlands.

It took the Dutch 39 years of policy changes to gradually reduce roadkills of children by 97%, to 14 in 2010. City Councils in the US compounded the problem making roads faster for cars and more unsafe for children.

The Divergence

Same starting point in 1973. Radically different outcomes. Per-capita oil use diverged by 60%. Pedestrian and cyclist death rates diverged by 400%.
