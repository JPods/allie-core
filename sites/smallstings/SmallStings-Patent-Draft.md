## TITLE OF THE INVENTION

Citizen-Initiated Micro-Fiscal Accountability System with Three-Dimensional Institutional Performance Rating and Automated Escalating Penalty Structure

---

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

This invention relates to systems and methods for real-time accountability between citizens and institutions (both governmental and commercial), specifically a system that creates fiscal events initiated by citizens to record institutional defects, tracks institutional responsiveness to those events, measures repetition of defects at geographic locations, and produces a three-dimensional accountability rating that no existing feedback, review, or complaint system generates.

---

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### The Problem: Accountability Is Batch-Processed

Every existing system for holding institutions accountable operates after the damage is done. Yelp reviews are written after the customer leaves. 311 calls enter a queue that may or may not produce action. SeeClickFix reports are routed to municipal departments with no fiscal consequence for ignoring them. Google Reviews provide retrospection after the relationship is over. Lawsuits arrive after someone is injured or killed.

All of these systems share a fatal structural deficiency: they separate the signal from the consequence. A citizen can report a dangerous crosswalk. The city can acknowledge the report. Neither event creates a fiscal record that the institution knew about the hazard. When someone is killed at that crosswalk six months later, the city claims ignorance. There is no constructive knowledge — no dated, geolocated, financially recorded chain of evidence that the institution was informed and failed to act.

The result is predictable and measurable. The United States Department of Transportation (DOT) system, which operates under sovereign immunity — meaning citizens cannot hold it accountable through jury trial for design failures — produces injury rates of approximately 11,200 per million people per year. Theme parks, which operate under full jury accountability through ASTM F24 safety standards, produce injury rates of approximately 3.4 per million people per year. This is a factor of 3,000. The difference is not engineering capability. The difference is accountability.

Morgantown Personal Rapid Transit (PRT), which has operated since 1975 under accountability standards equivalent to theme parks, has logged 54 years of operation with zero serious passenger injuries. During the same period, United States roads killed approximately 1.8 million Americans.

The mechanism that produces this difference is not regulation. It is not compliance. It is judgment — the judgment of juries, composed of ordinary citizens, evaluating whether an institution met its duty. Sovereign immunity removes that judgment from the equation for government-operated infrastructure. The result is 3,000 times more injuries.

### What Existing Systems Miss

**Yelp, Google Reviews, TripAdvisor** — These are retrospection platforms. They record what happened after the customer has left the premises. They contain no fiscal event. They measure satisfaction on a single dimension (was the experience good or bad). They do not measure whether the institution responded to complaints. They do not track whether the same complaint recurs at the same location. They provide no constructive knowledge record. A restaurant with 47 one-star reviews mentioning a tripping hazard at the same doorstep has no legal record of constructive knowledge — only informal public complaints with no fiscal weight.

**311 systems, SeeClickFix, FixMyStreet** — These route citizen reports to municipal departments. They create work orders. They do not create fiscal events. A city that receives 200 reports about a dangerous intersection and ignores all 200 faces no escalating consequence from the reporting system itself. The reports have no financial weight. They are requests, not records of institutional knowledge with fiscal consequences.

**Customer service hotlines and chatbots** — These are institution-controlled channels. The institution decides whether to acknowledge the complaint, how to categorize it, and whether to record it. The citizen has no independent record. The institution can delete, reclassify, or lose the complaint. There is no independent audit trail.

**Insurance claims and lawsuits** — These are the catastrophic end of the accountability spectrum. They arrive after injury, death, or significant financial loss. They are expensive, slow, adversarial, and binary (the institution either pays or does not). They provide no signal during the period when the defect could have been fixed at minimal cost. A lawsuit over a fatal crosswalk accident costs the city millions. The 47 Small Stings that would have fixed the crosswalk for $47 were never available as a mechanism.

### What No Existing System Provides

No existing system combines all three of the following:

1. **A fiscal event initiated by the citizen** — not a review, not a report, not a complaint, but a financial record that the institution was informed of a defect, with a specific dollar amount attached.

2. **A measurement of institutional responsiveness** — did the institution respond? How fast? Did they resolve the problem? Was the resolution satisfactory? This dimension is entirely absent from Yelp, Google Reviews, 311 systems, and every other existing feedback mechanism.

3. **A measurement of repetition at geographic locations** — is the same defect recurring at the same GPS coordinates? Is the pattern escalating? This dimension transforms individual complaints into constructive knowledge of systemic failure, with fiscal consequences that increase proportionally to the institution's failure to respond.

The combination of these three dimensions produces a score that answers a question no existing system can answer: **Does this institution learn from feedback and fix problems, or does it absorb complaints without changing?**

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention, referred to as "Small Stings," is a system and method for real-time accountability between citizens (or customers) and institutions (governmental or commercial) that creates micro-fiscal events, measures institutional responsiveness, tracks defect repetition at geographic locations, and produces a three-dimensional accountability rating.

The system operates on the principle that accountability should be continuous, proportional, and fiscally real — not retrospective, binary, and catastrophic. A $1 sting filed at the moment a citizen encounters a defect is a better accountability mechanism than a $1 million lawsuit filed after someone is killed by the same defect.

The core innovation is the **Triangle Rating System** — a three-dimensional measurement that evaluates institutions on:

1. **Value Signal** — The citizen's initiated fiscal event: what is wrong, where (GPS coordinates), when (timestamp), and photographic evidence. Each signal creates a financial record of constructive knowledge.

2. **Responsiveness** — The institution's response: whether they responded, how quickly, whether they resolved the defect, and whether the resolution was satisfactory. This dimension is tracked, rated, and published.

3. **Repetition** — The pattern dimension: recurring fiscal events at the same geographic coordinates. One sting is a complaint. Forty-seven stings at the same location is constructive knowledge of a systemic failure, with escalating fiscal consequences.

The system further includes:

- Automated escalating penalties for repeated unresolved defects at the same location
- AI-based routing of accountability signals to the specific organizational unit or individual capable of resolution
- Aggregation of geolocated defect reports to generate infrastructure design inputs (station placement, barrier placement, crosswalk redesign)
- A closed feedback loop where institutional response (or absence of response) is itself tracked, rated, and published
- Dual-mode operation for both public safety (citizen-to-government) and commercial service (customer-to-business)
- Integration with sovereign local commerce systems where the institution maintains data sovereignty while participating in the accountability network
- A retrospection payment system where institutions pay citizens for structured feedback reports, applying the usufruct principle to customer intelligence

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### 1. System Architecture

The Small Stings system comprises the following components:

**1.1 Citizen Mobile Application**

A mobile application through which citizens initiate fiscal events ("stings"). Each sting captures:

- GPS coordinates of the defect location (automatic, from device sensors)
- UTC timestamp of the filing (automatic)
- Photographic evidence (captured by the citizen via device camera)
- Text description of the defect
- Category classification (safety, service, infrastructure, inventory, cleanliness, accessibility)
- Citizen identity via a portable, owner-controlled digital identity system (the citizen owns their sting history; no platform controls or limits their records)

The mobile application stores the citizen's complete sting history on the citizen's own device and/or portable identity store. The platform does not own the citizen's data. The citizen can export, transfer, or delete their records at any time. This is a structural design choice, not a feature — the system applies the principle that the individual is sovereign over their own records.

**1.2 Routing Engine (Alice)**

An AI-based routing system that receives each sting and directs it to the specific organizational unit, employee, or system capable of resolving the defect. The routing engine:

- Identifies the institution responsible for the geographic location of the sting (city department, store, facility manager)
- Routes the sting to the most specific resolver available (e.g., not "City of Tulsa" but "Tulsa Public Works, District 3, Signal Maintenance")
- Provides the institution with a time window to respond before the fiscal event triggers
- Tracks response status in real time
- Detects patterns across multiple stings (same location, same defect category, same institution)
- Generates automated alerts when sting density at a location crosses defined thresholds

**1.3 Institution Response Portal**

A portal through which institutions receive, respond to, and resolve stings. The portal operates in two configurations:

- **Cloud-hosted portal** — The institution receives stings through a web dashboard hosted by the Small Stings platform. Suitable for institutions without their own server infrastructure.

- **Sovereign server integration** — The institution operates its own server (using a Desktop Hosting model or equivalent sovereign commerce platform) and receives stings directly on its own infrastructure via API. The sting data resides on the institution's server. The institution maintains complete data sovereignty while participating in the accountability network. The platform never becomes the intermediary that owns the relationship between citizen and institution.

**1.4 Triangle Rating Engine**

The computational engine that produces the three-dimensional accountability rating for each institution. Described in detail in Section 2 below.

**1.5 Constructive Knowledge Ledger**

A tamper-evident record of all stings filed against an institution, including timestamps, GPS coordinates, photographic evidence, response status, and resolution status. This ledger serves as a legal record of constructive knowledge — evidence that the institution was informed of a defect at a specific location on a specific date and either responded or failed to respond.

The ledger is structured so that each entry is independently verifiable: the GPS coordinates, timestamp, and photographic evidence are captured at the moment of filing and cannot be retroactively modified by either party. This creates an evidentiary chain suitable for use in legal proceedings, insurance claims, and regulatory actions.

### 2. The Triangle Rating System

The Triangle Rating System is the core innovation of the present invention. It measures institutional accountability on three dimensions simultaneously, producing a composite score that no existing system generates.

**2.1 Dimension One: Value Signal**

The Value Signal is the citizen's initiated fiscal event. When a citizen files a sting, the following occurs:

- A micro-fiscal event of a defined amount (initially $1.00) is created
- The event is geolocated (GPS coordinates accurate to the resolution of the citizen's device)
- The event is timestamped (UTC, to the second)
- The event includes photographic evidence captured by the citizen
- The event includes the citizen's description of the defect
- The event is categorized (safety, service, infrastructure, inventory, cleanliness, accessibility, other)

The Value Signal is not a review. It is not a rating on a 1-5 scale. It is a financial record. The $1.00 is not a payment for the citizen's time — it is a fine assessed against the institution for a defect that the citizen has identified. The financial nature of the event distinguishes it from every existing feedback mechanism and creates the evidentiary foundation for constructive knowledge.

**2.2 Dimension Two: Responsiveness**

The Responsiveness dimension measures the institution's behavior after receiving a sting. This dimension is entirely absent from existing review and feedback platforms. It tracks:

- **Acknowledgment time** — How quickly did the institution acknowledge the sting? Measured from sting filing to first institutional response. Acknowledgment requires a specific action (message to the citizen, assignment to a resolver, status change) — automated receipt confirmations do not constitute acknowledgment.

- **Resolution time** — How quickly did the institution resolve the defect? Measured from sting filing to resolution. Resolution requires evidence (photo of fixed defect, confirmation from citizen, verified status change).

- **Resolution quality** — Did the citizen confirm the resolution was satisfactory? The citizen has the option to confirm, reject, or not respond to the resolution. Rejected resolutions re-open the sting with the clock continuing.

- **Response rate** — What percentage of stings does the institution respond to? An institution that responds to 95% of stings within 24 hours has a fundamentally different Responsiveness score than one that responds to 30%.

The Responsiveness dimension produces a score for each institution that answers the question: "When citizens tell this institution about a problem, does the institution fix it?"

This is the dimension that transforms Small Stings from a complaint system into an accountability system. Yelp can tell you that a restaurant has problems. Small Stings can tell you whether the restaurant fixes problems when informed.

**2.3 Dimension Three: Repetition**

The Repetition dimension measures the recurrence of defects at the same geographic location. This is the pattern dimension — the dimension that transforms individual complaints into evidence of systemic failure.

The system tracks:

- **Geographic clustering** — Stings filed within a defined radius (initially 50 meters) of each other are treated as relating to the same location. The clustering radius is adjustable based on context (larger for highway segments, smaller for individual intersections or store aisles).

- **Temporal pattern** — The frequency of stings at a location over time. A location that receives one sting per month has a different Repetition profile than a location that receives 47 stings in one month.

- **Category persistence** — Are the stings at a location all reporting the same defect category? If 47 stings at an intersection all report "unmarked crosswalk," the Repetition signal is stronger than 47 stings reporting 47 different problems.

- **Escalating fiscal consequences** — The critical mechanism of the Repetition dimension. When stings repeat at the same geographic location without resolution, the fiscal value of each subsequent sting increases automatically:

  - Sting 1: $1.00
  - Sting 2 (same location, unresolved): $2.00
  - Sting 3: $4.00
  - Sting 4: $8.00
  - ...continuing geometric progression up to a defined cap

  The escalation creates economic pressure proportional to the institution's failure to respond. A single unresolved sting costs $1.00. Twenty unresolved stings at the same location cost thousands. The escalation ensures that institutions cannot simply absorb an infinite number of $1.00 stings without consequence.

- **Constructive knowledge threshold** — When the number of stings at a location crosses a defined threshold (initially 10), the system automatically generates a constructive knowledge notification to the institution's executive or governing authority, independent of the operational unit that has been receiving individual stings. This ensures that institutional leadership is informed of recurring defects even if the operational unit is ignoring or suppressing them.

**2.4 Triangle Score Computation**

The three dimensions combine to produce a Triangle Score for each institution:

```
Triangle Score = f(Value Signal density, Responsiveness rate, Repetition factor)
```

Where:
- Value Signal density = total stings per unit time, weighted by category severity
- Responsiveness rate = percentage of stings acknowledged and resolved within defined time windows
- Repetition factor = degree of geographic clustering and temporal persistence of unresolved stings

The Triangle Score answers the question that no existing system can answer: **"Does this institution learn from feedback and fix problems, or does it absorb complaints without changing?"**

An institution with high Value Signal density (many stings) but high Responsiveness (fast resolution) and low Repetition (problems don't recur) scores well — it operates in an environment where citizens actively report problems and the institution actively fixes them. This is the desired equilibrium.

An institution with high Value Signal density, low Responsiveness, and high Repetition scores poorly — citizens are reporting problems, the institution is not fixing them, and the same problems keep recurring. This is the pattern that the system is designed to make visible and fiscally consequential.

### 3. Automated Escalating Penalty System

When citizen-initiated fiscal events repeat at the same geographic location and the institution fails to resolve the underlying defect, the fiscal value of each subsequent event increases automatically according to a geometric progression.

The escalation serves three functions:

1. **Economic pressure** — An institution that ignores a $1.00 sting may choose to absorb the cost. An institution facing $1,024 in accumulated stings at the same location over a period of weeks has a financial incentive to fix the defect.

2. **Signal amplification** — The escalating cost makes chronic defects visible in financial reporting. A $1.00 sting is invisible in a municipal budget. Accumulated escalating stings at dangerous locations become a line item that cannot be ignored.

3. **Constructive knowledge escalation** — Each step in the escalation adds to the evidentiary record. An institution that pays $1.00 in sting fines has been informed once. An institution that pays $2,000 in escalating stings at the same crosswalk has been informed dozens of times and has documented knowledge of the hazard.

The escalation resets when the institution resolves the defect and the resolution is confirmed (by citizen confirmation or by absence of new stings at the location within a defined period).

### 4. Real-Time Routing and Pre-Resolution

The AI routing engine (Alice) directs each sting to the specific organizational unit or individual capable of resolving the defect. The routing occurs before the fiscal event fully triggers, creating a time window during which the institution can resolve the problem and avoid or reduce the sting.

In commercial mode, this produces a real-time service recovery mechanism:

- A customer in a store files a sting: "I can't find the toothpaste."
- Alice routes the sting to the nearest store employee's device within seconds.
- The employee responds: "Aisle 7, Shelf 3, left side."
- The customer confirms resolution. The sting is resolved. No fine accrues. The signal is recorded.

This transforms Small Stings from a punishment system into a service improvement system. The institution's fastest and cheapest response is to fix the problem before the sting becomes a fine. This is the correct incentive structure — it rewards responsiveness rather than penalizing failure after the fact.

### 5. Infrastructure Design Inputs from Aggregated Stings

The system aggregates geolocated citizen reports to automatically generate infrastructure design inputs. The density, geographic distribution, and category pattern of stings at geographic locations provides data suitable for:

- **Station placement** — A network design AI (Noelle) uses sting density as one input for determining optimal placement of transit stations. Locations with high sting density for safety-related defects (dangerous intersections, high-speed corridors, pedestrian hazards) indicate locations where grade-separated infrastructure would have the greatest impact.

- **Barrier placement** — Clusters of pedestrian safety stings along a corridor indicate locations where physical barriers or grade separation would prevent injuries.

- **Crosswalk and signal redesign** — Stings reporting unmarked crosswalks, broken signals, or blind intersections provide a continuous real-time map of infrastructure defects that can feed directly into municipal engineering priorities.

- **Commercial layout optimization** — In commercial mode, stings reporting inventory location problems, accessibility issues, or navigation difficulties provide data for store layout optimization.

The innovation is that the same data that drives accountability also drives infrastructure improvement. The citizens who are reporting defects are simultaneously providing the design input for fixing them.

### 6. Closed Feedback Loop

The system creates a closed loop between citizen reporters and institutions:

1. Citizen files a sting (Value Signal)
2. Institution receives the sting via routing engine
3. Institution responds (or fails to respond)
4. Response is tracked, timed, and rated (Responsiveness)
5. If the defect recurs, new stings file at the same location (Repetition)
6. The institution's Triangle Score updates continuously
7. The score is published and available to citizens, media, regulators, and peer institutions
8. The absence of response is itself a recorded and escalating fiscal event

Step 8 is critical. In every existing system, institutional silence is free. A city that receives 200 reports about a dangerous intersection and ignores all 200 faces no consequence from the reporting system itself. In Small Stings, institutional silence is a fiscal event. Each day without response at an active sting location adds to the Repetition dimension and the constructive knowledge ledger. Silence is not neutral — it is recorded.

### 7. Dual-Mode Operation

The system operates on the same infrastructure for both public safety and commercial service:

**Public Safety Mode (Citizen-to-Government)**
- Citizens file stings against government-operated infrastructure: roads, signals, crosswalks, sidewalks, lighting, public facilities
- Stings create constructive knowledge records against sovereign entities
- Sting patterns feed infrastructure design inputs
- Escalating fiscal consequences apply to government agencies
- Published Triangle Scores allow citizens to compare safety responsiveness across municipalities

**Commercial Mode (Customer-to-Business)**
- Customers file stings against commercial establishments: service failures, inventory problems, cleanliness, accessibility, wait times
- Real-time routing enables service recovery before the sting becomes a fine
- Sting patterns provide operational intelligence for store management
- Published Triangle Scores allow customers to evaluate businesses on responsiveness, not just satisfaction

Both modes use the same micro-fiscal event mechanism, the same routing system, the same Triangle Rating computation, and the same escalating penalty structure. The dual-mode architecture eliminates the artificial boundary between "citizen complaint" and "customer complaint" — both are instances of an individual holding an institution accountable through a fiscal event, and both benefit from the same three-dimensional measurement.

### 8. Integration with Sovereign Local Commerce Systems

The system is designed to integrate with sovereign local commerce platforms where the institution operates its own server infrastructure and maintains ownership of its own data.

In this integration model:

- The institution's server receives stings via API and processes them locally
- The institution's AI assistant (Alice) routes stings to specific employees and tracks resolution
- Sting data resides on the institution's server — not on a centralized platform
- The institution publishes its Triangle Score to the network voluntarily
- The citizen's sting record resides with the citizen — not on the institution's server or a centralized platform

This architecture ensures that no central platform becomes the owner or gatekeeper of the citizen-institution relationship. The platform provides the protocol and the routing infrastructure. The data remains sovereign to each party. This is a structural choice based on the principle that centralized platforms inevitably become the entity that citizens were trying to hold accountable — a feedback system that concentrates power defeats its own purpose.

### 9. Retrospection Payment System

The system includes a mechanism whereby institutions pay citizens for structured retrospection reports. A retrospection is a written account that includes:

- What the citizen expected
- What they experienced instead
- One specific change they would recommend

The retrospection payment applies the usufruct principle to customer intelligence: the citizen contributed value (diagnostic information about a defect); the institution returns value proportional to what was received (a payment for the citizen's time and judgment).

Retrospection payments are tracked alongside stings in the institution's accountability ledger. The ratio of stings-with-retrospections to stings-without-retrospections measures citizen engagement — a declining ratio indicates citizen disengagement, which is itself a signal of institutional unresponsiveness.

## ABSTRACT

A system and method for real-time accountability between citizens and institutions (governmental and commercial) using citizen-initiated micro-fiscal events, three-dimensional institutional performance rating, and automated escalating penalty structures.

Citizens use a mobile application to file defect reports ("stings") that are automatically geolocated (GPS), timestamped (UTC), and include photographic evidence. Each sting creates a micro-fiscal event — a financial record that the institution was informed of the defect. An AI routing engine directs each sting to the specific organizational unit or individual capable of resolution.

The core innovation is the Triangle Rating System, a three-dimensional accountability measurement comprising: (1) Value Signal — the density and severity of citizen-initiated fiscal events; (2) Responsiveness — the institution's response behavior including acknowledgment time, resolution time, and resolution quality; and (3) Repetition — the recurrence of unresolved defects at the same geographic location, with automatically escalating fiscal consequences.

Repeated unresolved stings at the same geographic location trigger automatic escalation of fiscal values, creating economic pressure proportional to the institution's failure to respond. The system operates in dual mode for both public safety (citizen-to-government) and commercial service (customer-to-business) using identical mechanisms. Integration with sovereign local commerce systems allows institutions to maintain data sovereignty while participating in the accountability network. Aggregated sting data generates infrastructure design inputs for transit station placement, barrier placement, and facility improvement. A retrospection payment system compensates citizens for structured feedback reports, applying the usufruct principle to customer intelligence.

The system creates the mechanism that existing review platforms, complaint systems, and reporting tools lack: a continuous, proportional, fiscally real accountability signal that forces institutional improvement before catastrophic failure — retrospection before the funeral.
