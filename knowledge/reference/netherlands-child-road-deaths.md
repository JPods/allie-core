**Source:** SWOV, ETSC PIN Flash Report 34, Streetsblog, Dutch Reach Project
**Last updated:** 2026-07-28

## Key Facts

- **Total Dutch road deaths peaked at 3,300 in 1971** (all ages)
- **~450 of those were children** — the trigger for the movement (sources range 400-500; we use ~450 per Streetsblog/Dutch Reach)
- **Steepest drop: first 20 years** (1971-1990, 500→~100) — infrastructure redesign period
- **Child risk now 1/20th of 1973 level** — even better than the overall 1/4 improvement for adults
- **Plateau since 2010** at ~14-18/year — suggests remaining deaths require next infrastructure shift (grade separation)
- **Overall road deaths 1970-1990:** 60% fewer in 20 years

## What Changed (1971-1990)

- Parents and children occupied accident blackspots
- Streets closed so children could play safely (woonerf concept)
- Designated bike lanes built systematically
- Speed limits reduced on residential streets
- Car-free zones created in city centers
- Government funding shifted from highways to cycling/pedestrian infrastructure
- School zones redesigned

## The Movement

- 1971: Simone Langenhoff, age 6, killed cycling to school
- Her father, journalist Vic Langenhoff, published "STOP DE KINDERMOORD" headline
- Thousands laid down with bicycles at the Rijksmuseum, pretending dead
- Protesters cycled with organ to PM Joop den Uyl's house singing for safer streets
- Government eventually funded the movement directly
